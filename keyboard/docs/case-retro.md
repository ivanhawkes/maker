# Case Design: Keyboard 2 (Retro)

Status: **generated 2026-11-23, digital checks only.** Nothing is printed, no part has been
measured, and no fit is proven. It is built round keyboard 1's insides
([case.md](case.md)), so everything said there about fits, the battery and the parts being
unmeasured applies here too.

This is the 1970s case of [decisions.md](decisions.md): an original design in the manner of
that decade's console hardware, for the white keycaps. It reproduces no show's logos, names or
props. It uses the same boards, layout and firmware as keyboard 1 and needs no board change.

The case is generated by `tools/case_retro_generate.py`; the files are in `cad/case-retro/`.

## What It Is

Five printed parts a half. Sizes: 215.6 × 136.8 mm in plan. On the desk the plate's top is
15.0 mm up at the near edge and 29.3 mm at the far edge; the hood's top is at 36.3 mm.

| Part | Colour | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tray | off-white | Keyboard 1's tray inside a body 6 mm thicker all round, with 10 mm corners and a rolled top edge |
| Plate | accent | Keyboard 1's plate, unchanged |
| Lid (the hood) | off-white | Keyboard 1's lid inside a taller body, 7 mm high, with a rolled edge and nine louvre grooves over the battery |
| Plinth | off-white | A raked base under the tray: 6°, far edge high. A stripe 2 mm high and 1.4 mm deep is recessed round its top |
| Stripe | accent | Optional. A ring 1.2 mm thick that fills the stripe; left out, the stripe is a shadow line |

**Colours (owner, 2026-11-23):** burnt orange for the accent, with brown and mustard also
approved. Avocado is not. The two colours are separate parts, so nothing needs multi-colour
printing.

**Filament (owner, 2026-11-23):** the colours come from Bambu Lab's filament range. PETG is
preferred; PLA will be considered if it gives more period-accurate colours. No filament is
chosen yet: the actual colours are to be picked from that range. The case is designed for the
PETG profile of [decisions.md](decisions.md); if a part is printed in PLA, check its fits again,
and note that PLA softens at a lower temperature than PETG, which matters beside the battery
and the charger ([case.md](case.md#limits-and-open-items)).

- **The rolled edge** is an arc of 6 mm radius from 45° to upright: 1.8 mm in and 4.2 mm
  high. It starts at 45° so that the hood, which prints top down, needs no support there. It is
  cut a print layer at a time, as keyboard 1's chamfers are.
- **The tray's top edge** is rolled where the hood does not sit; under the hood it is flat and
  the hood carries the roll.
- **The louvres** are 2 mm wide, 0.8 mm deep, 5 mm apart, across the hood. They do not go
  through.
- **The power switch** is reached through a notch 18 mm wide, cut through the new body from
  the hood's top down to the floor of keyboard 1's recess. It starts at the far end of the
  inner edge, so the body's rounded corner stays whole.
- **The USB tunnel** is keyboard 1's 13 mm for the first 4 mm and 16 mm wide through the new
  body.
- **The rake** was approved at 6° (owner, 2026-11-23). The plinth is a separate part because
  the tray must print flat: a raked tray printed on its base would have every seat inside it
  stepped.
- **Tenting:** keyboard 1's wedges do not fit this outline. Retro wedges are left for later
  (owner, 2026-11-23).

## Screws

Six a half, M3, from below, at keyboard 1's positions, each 4 mm longer because it also passes
through the plinth:

- four **M3 × 16** into the lid: two in the battery lobe, two through the board's holes H3, H4;
- two **M3 × 12** into the plate, through the board's holes H1 and H2.

The heads sit in recesses in the plinth, at least 3.9 mm deep, on a web 1.6 mm (lid) or 1.2 mm
(plate) thick with a 0.2 mm skin to drill out. The retro tray has no head recesses of its own,
so it cannot be closed without the plinth. The plinth also has six recesses for 10 mm stick-on
feet; the five in the tray are unused.

## Printing

Profile as for keyboard 1 ([decisions.md](decisions.md)). The slicer can add supports if a
first print shows they are needed (owner, 2026-11-23); none is designed in.

| Part | Orientation | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tray | floor down | The rolled top edge shows layer steps |
| Plate | top face down | As keyboard 1 |
| Lid | top face down | The louvres are on the bed: each is a 2 mm bridge, four layers up |
| Plinth | desk face down, as exported | Its walls lean 6° with the case. Open windows between ribs; its top is a slope |
| Stripe | flat | A thin closed ring; handle with care |

Volumes as solids: tray 100.2, plate 13.4, lid 32.2, plinth 91.8, stripe 1.3 cm³. The tray's
and lid's thick walls are for the slicer's infill to fill.

## Assembly

1. Assemble plate, board, battery, tray and lid as for keyboard 1, without screws.
2. Drop the stripe ring onto the step round the plinth's top.
3. Set the tray on the plinth and fit the six screws from below: the two short ones at H1 and
   H2, under the plate's top left and bottom middle.

## Generating And Checking

```sh
"$PY" tools/case_board.py                  # as for keyboard 1
FreeCADCmd tools/case_retro_generate.py    # about 4 minutes; exit 1 if a check fails
```

It builds keyboard 1's parts in memory with `tools/case_generate.py`, wraps them, mirrors the
result for the right half, and writes `<side>-<part>.step` (in keyboard 1's coordinates),
`<side>-case.FCStd`, `print/<side>-<part>.3mf` and `report.txt`.

**Results, 2026-11-23** (FreeCAD 1.1.4; `cad/case-retro/report.txt`). All pass, on both halves:

- Keyboard 1's checks, on all five parts: each is one valid solid and fits the bed; no two
  overlap; none touches the board or anything on it; 28 switch cutouts; the lid is 1.30 mm
  from the nearest keycap.
- The hood has nine louvre grooves, none within 3 mm of the reset hole.
- Every screw head has at least 3.9 mm of recess in the plinth.
- A press on any key, straight down or along the key's axis, lands inside the plinth's feet.
- The plinth as printed is 215.6 × 137.6 × 17.7 mm and stands flat.
- Each mesh is closed and has its solid's volume.

Also inspected by eye: renders from four sides and six sections (through H3, a lobe screw, the
controller and USB tunnel, the battery, and the power switch's notch).

## Limits And Open Items

None is tested. Keyboard 1's limits apply as well ([case.md](case.md#limits-and-open-items)).

- **The power switch is deeper in:** its slider is about 8 mm inside the body's face, at the
  bottom of the notch. A fingertip should reach it; that is a guess.
- **USB plug:** it now has to reach 10 mm into the body. Its overmould must be at most 13 mm
  wide at the tip and 16 mm behind that.
- **The front edge is higher:** 15.0 mm to the plate's top at the near edge, against 11.6 mm
  for keyboard 1 flat. Whether the rake is comfortable is for the owner to find out.
- **Thin webs under the screw heads** (1.2 and 1.6 mm). They only carry the plinth's weight;
  the tray's floor backs them when the screws are tightened.
- **The stripe ring** is 1.2 mm thick and held only by the step and the tray above it. Its
  0.2 mm fit is a guess.
- **Stability** is checked for where key presses land, not for weight: no mass is modelled.
- **Not designed:** retro tenting wedges; a retro dongle case.
